The Program: operator companies for treaty-national entrepreneurs, with Collective Horizon as service provider and minority partner

Résumé pour le fondateur. Ce document est le cahier des charges à remettre à trois conseils : un avocat de contrats et distribution, un avocat d'immigration, un avocat en valeurs mobilières. Il décrit le modèle retenu, la structure envisagée et les questions précises que chaque conseil doit trancher avant tout client payant et avant toute publicité. Rien dans ce document n'est un avis juridique : c'est la commande d'un avis juridique.

1. Business model overview

Collective Horizon ("CH") is a Miami based family office focused on American residential real assets. It intends to launch a services program ("the Program") for treaty-national entrepreneurs (primarily France, Belgium, Morocco) who wish to establish and personally direct their own US residential renovation company (fix and flip), typically capitalized at USD 100,000 to 200,000 by the client.

The client owns a majority of, and personally directs, their own LLC. Their immigration counsel may, where the client qualifies, prepare an E-2 treaty investor application founded on that enterprise. CH's role is strictly:

  1. Service provider to the client company: deal sourcing, underwriting, renovation project management through licensed contractors, resale coordination, reporting; for a program entry fee and per-project fees.
  2. Optional minority equity partner (20 to 40%) in the client company, subscribed in cash, documented in an operating agreement; the client remains majority owner and sole manager.

What the Program is not: CH does not pool or manage client funds, does not promise any return, does not promise any visa, and does not provide legal or immigration advice. Immigration work is performed by independent counsel engaged by the client. The Program is marketed separately from, and never jointly with, any co-investment activity of the family office.

3. Questions for corporate, franchise and distribution counsel

  1. Does the Program, as described, fall within the FTC Franchise Rule (trademark use, significant assistance, required payment)? If the client operates under their own brand with a services agreement only, is franchise classification avoided? What wording or structure keeps it that way?
  2. Does the FTC Business Opportunity Rule apply to the entry-fee offer? If so, prepare the required one-page disclosure and the seven-day sequence.
  3. Draft the master services agreement and fee schedule: scope, deliverables, term, termination, non-reliance and no-outcome clauses (no visa, no profit promised), limitation of liability.
  4. Draft or review the client LLC operating agreement where CH takes a minority stake: distributions, manager authority reserved to the client, buy-sell mechanics, deadlock, and an unwind clause if the client's visa application is refused or abandoned.
  5. Cash equity versus services-for-equity: any concerns (fraudulent transfer, valuation, tax) with CH receiving part of its stake against services?
  6. Dual role management: what disclosures and consent language cover CH acting simultaneously as paid service provider and minority member of the same LLC?
  7. Florida real estate licensing: which Program activities (sourcing properties for client companies for compensation, negotiating purchases or sales) require a Florida real estate licence, and how must the Program be structured or staffed to comply?
  8. Florida contractor licensing: confirm that renovation work is contracted directly between the client LLC and licensed general contractors, with CH in a coordination role only, and define the boundary that keeps CH outside contractor licensing requirements.
  9. Review all marketing materials before publication: no visa promises, no earnings claims, clear separation from the family office's investment activity.
  10. Recommend E&O and general liability coverage levels for CH Programs LLC.

4. Questions for immigration counsel

  1. Confirm the minimum ownership and control configuration for E-2 eligibility where CH holds a minority stake: is client 60 to 80% with sole manager status the safe harbor you recommend?
  2. For a renovation company capitalized at USD 100,000 to 200,000, what do you consider "substantial" and how much must be committed or spent before filing?
  3. Marginality: how do you weigh contractor spend versus direct hires? What hiring plan (roles, timing) makes the five-year business plan solid?
  4. Does CH's minority stake, subscribed in cash, strengthen or complicate the file at post?
  5. Current processing at Paris and Casablanca posts, and change-of-status alternatives for clients already in the US.
  6. Family coverage confirmation: spouse work authorization, children under 21; and the honest limits (non-immigrant status, renewal conditions) our materials must state.
  7. Renewal risk: what operating record (projects, revenue, hires) do you want to see at first renewal for this business model?
  8. Referral structure: what arrangement between CH and your firm is proper (client engages you directly; CH receives no share of legal fees)?

5. Questions for securities counsel

  1. Confirm that the Program, as structured (client-directed operating company, CH services and minority stake), does not involve the offer of a security under Howey and Florida law, and identify the features that would change that conclusion (degree of client passivity, pooled elements, profit-sharing wording).
  2. Confirm that public advertising of the Program as a service is permissible, and set the review process for campaigns.
  3. Define the separation rules between the Program and the family office's future 506(b) co-investment offering: websites, funnels, language, referrals between the two audiences, so that Program advertising cannot be attributed to the private offering.
  4. Review the referral relationships (immigration firms, real estate brokers) for compensation issues.
  5. Standing instruction: any future product where clients entrust funds to CH for investment returns is out of scope of the Program and requires a private placement memorandum before any discussion with prospects.
